Long-term investment thesis · NEAR · October 2026

NEAR is turning from a blockchain people build on into an exchange layer people route money through.

NEAR Intents lets anyone swap almost any token across 30-plus chains, and since 23 February 2026 the fees NEAR keeps from those swaps buy NEAR back on the open market. The long-term case comes down to one measurable question: can those buybacks grow faster than the new NEAR that inflation creates? Every number on this page is pulled live from public data, and the model behind the conclusion can be re-run by anyone.

The model: when do buybacks match inflation?

Volume compounds at a monthly growth rate. NEAR keeps its cut. That revenue buys NEAR at a given price. New NEAR is created at the inflation rate. Coverage is NEAR bought ÷ NEAR created; 100% means buybacks absorb all new supply. The three scenarios are built only from measured data, and the sliders let you test your own view.

What would prove this wrong

A thesis is only useful if it says how it fails. These are the risks, and where each would show up in the data first.

Routes go dark

NEAR Intents can pause a chain. On 29 Oct 2026 we observed the 1Click API refusing every deposit from Ethereum mainnet, and temporary minimums of $100 on USDT (Tron) and $1,000 on BNB Chain. Long pauses on big chains cut volume directly.

Shows up in: claim 1 (volume) within weeks.

Fee competition

Cross-chain swaps are a crowded market. If wallets and frontends route elsewhere, or NEAR lowers its fee to keep them, revenue per dollar of volume falls even while volume grows.

Shows up in: claim 2 (take rate).

Whale-driven volume

A handful of very large days can flatter a quarter. Volume that depends on a few traders or one integrator is fragile.

Shows up in: claim 3 (breadth).

Buybacks are held, not burned

Bought NEAR sits in a buyback wallet rather than being destroyed. A future decision could sell it, which would reverse the effect. Governance, not code, protects the policy.

Shows up in: the buyback wallet's balance and NEAR governance announcements.

The base chain keeps shrinking

NEAR blockchain activity is far below a year ago. Intents settles on NEAR, but if the chain's own ecosystem keeps fading, validators, developers and liquidity could thin out too.

Shows up in: chart 3 and the transactions tile above.

Data risk

Revenue and volume come from DefiLlama's adapters and NearBlocks. Methodology changes or missing days move the numbers. That is why rates use 90-day windows and every input is linked.

Shows up in: the Method section and the saved snapshots in the repository.

Method and sources

Definitions

  • Volume: dollar value of swaps settled through NEAR Intents.
  • Revenue: what NEAR itself captures from those swaps, measured as NEAR/wNEAR swept into its revenue wallets (reconciles with revenue.near.org). Gross fees are higher because integrators and solvers keep the rest.
  • Take rate: revenue ÷ volume over the last 90 days, in basis points (1 bp = 0.01%).
  • Buybacks: revenue used to buy NEAR on the open market since 23 Feb 2026 (DefiLlama "holders revenue").
  • Issuance: change in NEAR total supply over the last 365 days (NearBlocks), valued at today's price.
  • Coverage: yearly buybacks at the 90-day pace ÷ yearly issuance in dollars.

Re-run it yourself

git clone https://github.com/HATWHY4041/near-intents-thesis
cd near-intents-thesis
npm run model            # fetch fresh data, print the report, save a snapshot
npm run model -- --growth 0.02 --take-bps 4   # your own assumptions
npm start                # this page on http://localhost:3000

No API keys and nothing to install: Node 20 or newer is enough. Each run saves a dated snapshot in data/, so the evidence behind any version of the thesis stays checkable.
